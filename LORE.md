# Paper Planes world and lore bible

> "He couldn't deliver his own love in time, so he became the wind that carries everyone else's."

## 1. High concept and themes

Paper Planes takes place on a sky-bound island that used to be an ordinary cliffside homestead in the world below. A lone writer unearthed a forgotten relic there, poured his unspoken love into thousands of folded letters, and pulled his home loose from the earth.

Three themes anchor the setting:
- Unspoken words carry weight. What people refuse to say does not vanish; it lingers.
- Holding on too long costs the person doing it, even when it creates a shelter for others.
- A letter that never reaches its intended reader can still be heard by someone drifting through the same sky.

## 2. The Astral Inkstone

The island's magic comes from a single artifact: the Astral Inkstone.

### Origin and the forgotten Sky-Couriers
Long before modern roads or postal routes connected the world below, an order of Sky-Couriers (also called Wind-Scribes) carried urgent messages across mountains and seas. They forged Astral Inkstones from fallen meteoric glass and condensed sky-ore to bind written ink to upper-atmosphere wind currents.

An Inkstone was meant to be used sparingly, for one addressed letter at a time, so the wind knew where to drop it. The Writer's cliffside homestead happened to sit directly over a long-buried Courier outpost. Centuries after the Couriers faded into myth, the roots of his garden pond pushed the last surviving Inkstone back to the surface.

### Rules of the ink
1. Weightlessness of intent: Anything written in Astral Ink and folded into flight follows the writer's intent instead of gravity. It will not touch the ground until it reaches the person it was written for, or until the writer genuinely lets go.
2. The scribe's cost: Astral Inkstones were never meant to be used thousands of times by one person. Every time the Writer ground the stone, the ink drew a tiny piece of his breath, warmth, and physical weight to fuel the flight.
3. Saturation and uplift: Over years of writing, residue from the Inkstone soaked into the cottage floorboards, the garden soil, and the cliffside rock, slowly severing the land from the earth's pull and lifting it above the clouds.
4. Finite fuel and the core inscription: The Inkstone was not infinite. When it finally ran dry and cracked open, it revealed the Couriers' final rule carved into its hollow core:
   > "The ink only borrows the wind. When the stone runs dry, only the scribe's last breath can keep the current alive."

## 3. Chronological timeline

### The cliffside years
An ordinary man lived alone in a small A-Frame Cottage overlooking a valley. Every night he wrote letters about himself, about the people he watched from afar, and most of all about one woman he loved more than he knew how to say out loud.

Too afraid to send them, his desk overflowed. One night at the Campfire Terrace, he came close to burning the stack. Instead, he folded a single page into a paper plane and threw it off the cliff. It fell like ordinary paper, until the night he found the Inkstone.

### The discovery and the ascent
After unearthing the Astral Inkstone near his garden stream, he began rewriting his letters in its ink. When he folded the first inked page and cast it into the dark, it caught an unseen current and stayed aloft.

Night after night, he folded and launched another, hoping one would reach her. Because he never had the courage to write her true name or address on the pages, the planes could not land. They entered a permanent holding pattern around his home.

Over time, the accumulated magic lifted the entire cliffside (cottage, garden, pond, and campfire) free from the earth and into the upper sky.

### The beacon and the drift
Suspended above the clouds, he realized the mist was too thick for his planes to find a way down, or for anyone to find him. He built the Lantern Shrine to focus the Inkstone's residual glow into a steady beacon.

That beacon, combined with the island's upward current, began pulling in stray, unspoken thoughts from the world below. Anyone down on earth writing a confession, apology, or 2 AM wish they could not send found their words drawn upward into his sky.

### The sacrifice
Years blurred together. Because he had overused the Inkstone thousands of times, the Writer was already fading: his shadow thinned, his footsteps barely touched the grass, and his warmth leaked into the breeze. When the Astral Inkstone finally ran dry and cracked open, revealing the Couriers' inscription inside its core, the updraft began to fail.

By then, he understood that he had waited too long, and whatever life the woman had below had moved on without him. Still, he could not bear to let everyone else's unsent letters crash into nothingness.

Understanding the inscription, and already half-dissolved into the air, he went to the Lantern Shrine. He laid down his pen, placed one final unaddressed letter inside the lantern, and let go of what remained of his body, his voice, and his memory of her face. He dissolved into the living current of the island and became the Keeper of the Wind.

## 4. Key figures

### The Writer (Keeper of the Wind)
Before his transformation, he was an observant man paralyzed by the fear of ruining what little he had, or being rejected if he spoke the truth. Afterward, he ceased to be human or visible. He is now the steady breeze that circles the island, keeps the planes aloft, and carries newcomers' letters into the sky.

In becoming the wind so the updraft would never die, he lost his form and forgot his name, leaving only echoes behind: a warm mug in the cottage, a smoldering campfire, and scattered Original Letters.

### The Woman
Unseen throughout the story, she is the person for whom the island was accidentally built. Whether she moved away, passed on, or waited for a word that never came is left ambiguous so the player can piece it together from fragments of his letters. Her only physical trace on the island is a pink ribbon left behind long ago.

### The Frog
Originally an ordinary pond frog living in the garden before the cliffside tore free from the earth, it was touched by the Inkstone's magic seeping into the water and stopped aging. It is the only living creature on the island that still remembers the Writer's voice and saw the Woman's face.

The pink bow it wears belonged to her. One lonely afternoon, the Writer tied it gently to his only companion so he would still have a piece of her color in the garden. Later, when the island rose and the garden stream became an endless waterfall pouring into the clouds, he worried the frog would be swept over the edge and placed it safely in a toy duck floatie. To visitors, the frog sounds like a cheerful pond mascot (`"the shrine is so cozy..."`, `"did a wish fly by?"`), even though it is still waiting by the water for a man who turned into the wind.

## 5. Geography and landmarks

### On the island
| Landmark | Lore and significance |
| :--- | :--- |
| A-Frame Cottage | His former home, now the silent anchor of the island. Inside sits his writing desk, stacks of blank paper, and the cracked, empty Astral Inkstone. |
| Campfire Terrace | Where he sat on sleepless nights, and where he nearly burned his letters before deciding to fold them instead. Its embers never fully go cold, warming the air for updrafts. |
| Lantern Shrine | Built by the Writer to stabilize the Inkstone's wild magic into a lighthouse above the clouds. It is both the magnetic heart of the wind and the site of his final sacrifice. |
| Frog Pot and Garden | Where the Astral Inkstone was originally unearthed beneath the roots, and where the timeless Frog still keeps watch. |
| Waterfall Stream | The closed-loop water cycle of the island. Water pours off the cliff into the updraft, turns to mist, and returns as rain, dew, and snow so the sanctuary never dries out. |

### Beyond the island
Beneath the sea of clouds lies the World Below, the ordinary earth where the Writer and the Woman lived, and where an empty cliffside scar marks where his land tore away. Farther out in the fog lies the Outer Drift: if one forgotten Inkstone could lift a homestead, other relics or fragments of unspoken memory may drift beyond the horizon as smaller islets, lone benches, or broken docks.

## 6. Atmosphere and weather

Time on the island follows the emotional seasons the Writer spent at his desk rather than a normal calendar. Dawn, morning, and afternoon hold the hopeful hours when he still believed he might find the courage to go down the mountain. Dusk, evening, and 2 AM midnight are when the Lantern Shrine glows brightest and the heaviest truths are written. Rain and snow form as the closed-loop mist from the waterfall condenses back onto the island, echoing the long, isolated seasons he spent indoors writing.

## 7. Open narrative hooks

1. The island's name: Cathexis, named for the pouring of emotional energy into a single person or idea, though the Writer simply called it Home.
2. Who the player is: Either a Traveler from below who wrote something they could not send and woke up on the island's shore, or the Courier tasked with sorting through the sky's noise, recovering the Writer's numbered Original Letters, and deciding what happens to his final unaddressed plane.
3. The ending choice: Closure (delivering the Writer's final letter through the clouds, releasing the Keeper of the Wind at the risk of letting the island return to the earth) or Preservation (leaving the final letter in the Lantern Shrine to keep the wind alive so strangers' words can fly forever).
